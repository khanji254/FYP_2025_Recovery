\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

The Nakuja N-4 rocket recovery system addresses three critical mechatronic
failure modes documented in preceding missions: insufficient telemetry
communication range, uncontrolled nichrome-wire igniter burnout during
pyrotechnic parachute deployment, and an absence of real-time pre-flight
electrical status feedback. This report details the complete design, fabrication,
validation, and field testing of the mechatronic recovery suite developed to
resolve these systemic challenges.

The telemetry subsystem utilises a Digi XBee-PRO 900HP transceiver operating
within the 900\,MHz ISM band under transparent Universal Asynchronous
Receiver-Transmitter (UART) protocol. A first-order link-budget analysis
validates a robust link margin of 32.34\,dB over a 5\,km line-of-sight recovery
baseline. Field range testing confirmed sustained bidirectional packet delivery at
distances exceeding 5\,km, with battery voltage, altitude, and RSSI telemetry
verified live on the ground-station liquid-crystal display (LCD).

The deployment ignition subsystem incorporates low-side switching driven by
logic-level IRLZ44N Power MOSFETs regulated via a calibrated 70\% Pulse-Width
Modulation (PWM) scheme. Dual deployment pop tests confirmed successful drogue
and main parachute ejection without nichrome wire burnout under repeated
firings. System diagnostic feedback is provided via a 16-bit ADS1115
Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC) over Inter-Integrated Circuit (I2C),
supplying ground operators with real-time loop continuity verification and
battery bus voltage tracking with a reconstructed accuracy of $\pm$0.02\,V.

Mechanically, an additive-manufactured stepped Polyethylene Terephthalate
Glycol (PETG) avionics bay sled secures four parallel 18650 lithium-ion cells
within the 95\,mm inner airframe diameter. A drone-mounted simulated-flight
test confirmed active telemetry and correct FSM state sequencing. All
mechatronic subsystems have been fabricated, integrated, and field-validated,
establishing a reproducible high-power rocket recovery standard for the Nakuja
programme.
