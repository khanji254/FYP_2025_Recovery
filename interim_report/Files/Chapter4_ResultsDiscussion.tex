\section{Results and Discussion}\label{sec:results}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Overview}

This chapter presents the results obtained during the current phase of the project and discusses their implications. Work completed to date covers: a performance audit of the baseline system that established the root causes of its limitations; targeted improvements to the sorting mechanism including a camera replacement, transistor upgrades, and ESP32 co-processor integration; circuit verification by Proteus simulation; and initial model development using a newly compiled binary dataset on the upgraded Raspberry Pi~4 platform.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Performance Analysis of Existing System}
\label{subsec:obj1results}

A detailed performance audit of the existing sorting machine was conducted to establish a baseline and identify the root causes of its documented limitations. The key findings are presented in Table~\ref{tab:baseline}:

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{Measured baseline performance of existing sorting machine}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Measured Value} & \textbf{Target} \\ \midrule
    End-to-end pipeline latency & $\approx 380$\,ms & $<100$\,ms \\
    Overall classification accuracy & $\approx 72\%$ & $>90\%$ \\
    Throughput (berries/min) & $\approx 35$ & $>80$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \label{tab:baseline}
\end{table}

The dominant contribution to latency was the serialised inference on the Pi~3
($\approx 310$\,ms per frame using TensorFlow Lite MobileNet). Motion blur from
the rolling-shutter camera degraded image quality at operating chute speed, reducing
classification confidence. Misclassification was concentrated at the semi-ripe/ripe
boundary in the four-class model, where visually similar colours produced ambiguous
predictions. These findings directly motivated the improvements described in the
following sections.

\subsection{Sorting Mechanism Improvements}
\label{subsec:obj2results}

Three targeted upgrades were implemented to address the root causes identified in the performance audit:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Camera replacement:} The rolling-shutter Raspberry Pi Camera V2 was
        replaced with the \textbf{Arducam OV9281 Global-Shutter Module} (CSI-2,
        1\,MP, Sony OV9281). Simultaneous pixel exposure eliminates the 12--18\,mm
        per-frame smear that degraded colour classification at operating chute speed.
  \item \textbf{Ejector driver transistor upgrade:} PN2222A BJTs were replaced with
        \textbf{IRLZ44N logic-level MOSFETs}. The MOSFET has no minority-carrier
        storage time, giving sub-20\,ns turn-on versus $\approx$100\,ns + 200--600\,ns
        storage for the BJT. Full drive is achieved direct from the ESP32 3.3\,V GPIO
        with negligible steady-state gate current.
  \item \textbf{ESP32 co-processor:} An ESP32 microcontroller was introduced to handle
        all hard real-time tasks---solenoid pulse timing, motor PWM, relay switching---via
        deterministic hardware timers ($<$5\,\textmu{}s ISR latency), relieving the
        Raspberry Pi Linux scheduler from GPIO-critical paths.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Procured and Verified Hardware}

\subsubsection{Proteus Circuit Simulation Results}

Prior to physical construction, the ESP32 control circuit was fully simulated in
Proteus ISIS to verify all subsystems before hardware was assembled.

\paragraph{Full Circuit Overview}

Figure~\ref{fig:proteus_full} shows the complete Proteus schematic: four IRLZ44N
ejector channels, vibrating motor PWM stage, LED strip driver, 2-channel optocoupler
relay module, and UART virtual terminal representing the Raspberry Pi link.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{Full_proteus_circuit.pdf}
  \caption{Complete Proteus ISIS simulation: ESP32 with four IRLZ44N solenoid driver
  channels, vibrating motor PWM, LED strip driver, 2-channel optocoupler relay, and
  UART virtual terminal}
  \label{fig:proteus_full}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{UART Communication Verification}

The serial link between the virtual Raspberry Pi terminal and the ESP32 was verified at
115\,200\,bps (8N1). Binary command strings (\texttt{REJECT:1}---\texttt{REJECT:4},
\texttt{ACCEPT}) injected via the virtual terminal were correctly parsed by the ESP32
firmware. Acknowledgement responses were returned within the simulated 10\,ms target
latency.

\paragraph{Solenoid Driver Switching Waveforms}

Figures~\ref{fig:trig_on} and~\ref{fig:trig_off} show oscilloscope captures of the
MOSFET turn-on and turn-off events respectively.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{Trigger_ON.PNG}
  \caption{Proteus oscilloscope: MOSFET turn-ON --- gate rises to 3.3\,V and drain
  falls from 12\,V to $\approx$0\,V with no ringing (IRLZ44N, 220\,$\Omega$ gate
  resistor, 100\,k$\Omega$ pull-down)}
  \label{fig:trig_on}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{Trigger_OFF.PNG}
  \caption{Proteus oscilloscope: MOSFET turn-OFF --- drain recovers to 12\,V;
  1N5819 clamps inductive kickback spike within 0.5\,V of the supply rail}
  \label{fig:trig_off}
\end{figure}

Key results from the waveform analysis:
\begin{itemize}
  \item All four MOSFET channels switched correctly on receipt of \texttt{REJECT:n} commands.
  \item No gate ringing observed; 220\,$\Omega$ series resistor adequately damped parasitic oscillation.
  \item Flyback diode clamped solenoid turn-off spike to +12.5\,V (0.5\,V above rail).
  \item 100\,k$\Omega$ pull-down confirmed zero false firing during ESP32 power-on reset.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Motor PWM and Compressor Relay}

PWM on GPIO~14 swept 0--100\% produced proportional average voltage across the motor
load, confirming correct vibrating-feeder speed control. The GPIO~21 relay cycled the
compressor load ON/OFF with clean switching and correct protection diode behaviour.

\subsection{Hardware Upgrade and Model Development}
\label{subsec:obj3results}

With the mechanism improvements validated, work shifted to upgrading the processing
platform and classification model. The \textbf{Raspberry Pi~4 (4\,GB RAM)}
replaces the Pi~3B+, providing $\approx$3$\times$ memory bandwidth and enabling
YOLOv11 inference to overlap with the next frame capture. In parallel, the
MobileNetV2 four-class classifier was retired; \textbf{YOLOv11n} was retrained
on a new 2{,}000-image binary dataset, eliminating the semi-ripe/ripe boundary
confusion that limited the original system. The performance shortcomings of the reference four-class model that directly motivated this dataset redesign are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:dataset}, Figures~\ref{fig:app_old_f1} and~\ref{fig:app_old_val}.

\subsubsection{Dataset and Class Reduction}

The original four-class dataset was discarded and a new \textbf{2{,}000-image binary
dataset} was compiled. Images were captured on the physical chute under operational
illumination and annotated on Roboflow with two labels only: \texttt{accept}
(fully ripe, deep-red) and \texttt{reject} (all other states). Consolidating four
ambiguous classes into two eliminates the dominant misclassification boundary.
