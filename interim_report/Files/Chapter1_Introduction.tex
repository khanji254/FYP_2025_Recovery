\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Background of Study}

The global coffee industry remains a significant contributor to the economies of many developing countries, supporting millions of smallholder farmers worldwide. Coffee production involves several critical stages, from cultivation and harvesting to processing and packaging. Among these stages, the sorting of coffee berries is one of the most labor-intensive and quality-sensitive processes. Traditionally, coffee berry sorting has relied heavily on manual visual inspection, where workers separate berries based on ripeness, size, and visible defects. This approach is time-consuming, inconsistent, and costly, often leading to variations in coffee quality and increased operational expenses.

Manual sorting methods are highly susceptible to human error and fatigue, which can result in misclassification of berries and reduced overall product quality. Furthermore, the labor-intensive nature of manual sorting limits scalability and efficiency, particularly during peak harvest periods when labor shortages are common. These challenges highlight the need for automated sorting solutions capable of performing classification tasks with higher accuracy, consistency, and throughput.

Recent advancements in computer vision, machine learning, and embedded systems have enabled the development of automated coffee berry sorting machines that utilize image-based detection and real-time classification techniques. Previous work by fifth-year mechatronics engineering students resulted in the development of an image-based coffee berry sorting machine incorporating a vibrating conveyor system, optical detection, and air-ejection mechanisms controlled by a Raspberry Pi-based processing unit. While the system successfully demonstrated automated sorting, practical challenges were identified during real-time operation, particularly related to motion blur, processing latency, and limited computational throughput.

This project proposes the modification and improvement of the existing coffee berry sorting machine by addressing the identified limitations in image acquisition, processing speed, and real-time classification performance. By enhancing the vision processing pipeline, optimizing algorithms, and evaluating alternative hardware or processing architectures, the project aims to improve sorting accuracy and throughput while maintaining a cost-effective and scalable design.

\subsection{Problem Statement}

The quality of coffee berries at the processing stage directly determines the final product grade, market value, and competitiveness in both local and international markets. Despite the successful demonstration of automated sorting in the previous system, it exhibits several critical technical limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Motion blur and illumination inconsistency:} Dynamic motion of berries on the conveyor and variable ambient lighting introduce image degradation, reducing classification accuracy.
  \item \textbf{Processing latency:} The limited computational capacity of the Raspberry Pi 3 causes delays across the frame acquisition, preprocessing, inference, and actuation pipeline, constraining throughput.
  \item \textbf{Model accuracy and class confusion:} The original four-class model confused visually similar semi-ripe and ripe berries, causing commercially acceptable red berries to be rejected and semi-ripe berries to pass into the accept stream, reducing economic yield.
\end{enumerate}

There is a clear need to improve the vision system, processing architecture, and real-time control strategy to achieve higher throughput, lower latency, and improved classification accuracy.

\subsection{Objectives}

\subsubsection{Main Objective}

To modify and improve an existing automated coffee berry sorting machine.

\subsubsection{Specific Objectives}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item To analyse the performance limitations of the existing image-based coffee berry sorting system, with emphasis on motion blur, processing latency, and classification accuracy.
  \item To improve on the sorting mechanism.
  \item To evaluate alternative or upgraded processing hardware capable of supporting higher frame rates and faster inference.

\subsection{Justification}

Coffee remains one of Kenya's most valuable agricultural exports, and quality grading plays a major role in determining its economic value. Automated sorting systems offer a practical solution for improving quality consistency and reducing labour dependency. Improving the existing image-based system provides a cost-effective pathway by building upon already validated concepts while addressing observed technical shortcomings.

The proposed improvements are expected to:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Enhance accuracy and throughput in coffee berry sorting.
  \item Enable better utilisation of affordable embedded vision technologies.
  \item Increase the feasibility of locally developed agri-tech solutions.
  \item Contribute practical learning and innovation in mechatronics system integration.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope of the Project}

This project is limited to the modification and improvement of the existing coffee berry sorting machine. Specifically:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The mechanical conveyor and air-ejection hardware remain unchanged from the original design unless critical faults are identified.
  \item Focus is placed on the vision and control sub-systems: camera selection, lighting, model training, inference pipeline, and microcontroller–Pi communication.
  \item Classification uses a binary \textbf{ACCEPT/REJECT} scheme: the \texttt{accept} class covers uniform deep-red ripe berries; the \texttt{reject} class covers unripe (green), semi-ripe (yellow/orange), overripe (dark/black), and physically damaged berries. This aligns with Kenyan cooperative grading practice.
  \item Deployment targets the Raspberry Pi~4 or equivalent single-board computer for embedded inference.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Expected Outcomes}

Upon completion of this project, the following outcomes are expected:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A YOLOv11-based coffee berry detection model achieving binary classification accuracy $>$90\% (mAP@0.5) on the \texttt{accept}/\texttt{reject} scheme, replacing the existing MobileNetV2 classifier.
  \item An end-to-end sorting pipeline latency of less than 100\,ms (detection to solenoid trigger), achieved through the Pi~4 + ESP32 co-processing architecture.
  \item Sorting throughput exceeding 150 berries per minute, an improvement over the baseline of approximately 35 berries per minute.
  \item A validated Proteus circuit simulation of the ESP32 control circuit prior to physical implementation, confirming correct solenoid switching, UART timing, and motor PWM behaviour.
  \item A fully characterised and validated sorting system with documented design drawings, test results, and a reproducible codebase.
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Note: These expected outcomes are defined here, prior to system implementation. Actual results and performance comparisons are presented in Chapter~4.}
