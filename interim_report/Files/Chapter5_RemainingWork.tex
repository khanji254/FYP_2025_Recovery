\section{Conclusion and Recommendations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\subsection{Conclusion}

This project has demonstrated a fully realised, structurally verified mechatronic
solution for the N-4 sounding rocket dual-deployment recovery system,
systematically mitigating the legacy constraints of telemetry range limits,
pyrotechnic ignition fatigue, and the absence of pre-flight diagnostics.

The integration of the sub-GHz Digi XBee-PRO 900HP transceiver was successfully
achieved, verifying a deterministic hardware serial data link. Mathematical
link-budget profiling confirmed a robust link margin of 32.34\,dB for the 5\,km
line-of-sight recovery baseline, validated empirically during field range testing
with the ground-station LCD confirming live packet reception including battery
voltage and RSSI.

Controlled energy delivery across the deployment channels was validated through
IRLZ44N MOSFET switching with 70\% PWM duty cycle. Dual deployment pop tests
confirmed successful drogue and main parachute ejection without nichrome wire
structural failure, establishing a reliable alternative to direct-current discharge
methods.

Pre-flight systemic feedback was established via the 16-bit ADS1115 ADC over I$^2$C.
Empirical bench testing confirmed voltage reconstruction accuracy of $\pm$0.02\,V
across the full operating range, enabling automated continuity checks and
launch-inhibit safety logic.

The 3D-printed PETG avionics bay sled satisfied all physical constraints within
the 95\,mm inner airframe diameter. The stepped battery seating geometry bypassed
internal threaded structural rod trajectories while providing vibration isolation
and secure battery retention under launch and pop-test loading.

The embedded firmware was deployed as a concurrent multi-threaded architecture
under FreeRTOS on the ESP32 microcontroller. A drone-mounted simulated flight
test confirmed active telemetry and correct FSM state sequencing under dynamic
conditions. The N-4 mechatronic recovery suite provides a scalable and robust
design methodology for student-led aerospace initiatives.

\subsection{Recommendations for Future Work}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Non-Volatile Black-Box Data Logging:} Integrate a high-speed SPI
    micro-SD card logging module within the FreeRTOS framework to provide full
    onboard data redundancy against transient telemetry dropouts at apogee.

  \item \textbf{Advanced Sensor Fusion for Apogee Detection:} Incorporate a
    dual-sensor filtering matrix (BMP280 barometer + MPU6050 accelerometer)
    within the FSM logic to prevent premature ejection triggers caused by
    transonic pressure anomalies.

  \item \textbf{Bi-Quad Base-Station Antenna Upgrade:} Replace the duck dipole
    ground-station antenna with a directional Bi-Quad at 900\,MHz to improve
    null-zone robustness and extend effective link range during descent phases
    when the rocket antenna axis may align with the ground-station direction.

  \item \textbf{Full Rocket Integration and Launch:} Complete all hardware
    integration, conduct a final end-to-end rehearsal test, and proceed to a
    supervised launch at the Isiolo School of Infantry Range as an entry into the
    Spaceport America Cup.

  \item \textbf{Parachute Sizing and Drop Test:} Validate drogue and main
    parachute sizing against the N-4 target descent rate profile using a drop
    test from a tethered balloon or elevated platform.
\end{enumerate}
